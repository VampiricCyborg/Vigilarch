append-only incident ledger · no server · no consensus · no trusted clock

A meeting is
the proof of time.

An append-only incident ledger that can defend when a record was created, with no server, no consensus, and no trusted clock.

Two nodes meet offline and co-sign each other’s current chain head. That signature is an upper bound — the record existed no later than the meeting; it never proves the record did not exist earlier. Where no meeting covers a record, the answer is unwitnessed, and the window stays open. spec/02 §5.2, §8.1

the problem · sourced, and deliberately without statistics

Four things go wrong when the network is usually down.

Vigilarch starts from the premise that the partition is the normal state, not the failure state. Each problem below is one this repository names in a specification or an invariant. There are no field numbers here because there is no field: this is a public repository with a self-contained demo, not a product.

Capture loss

A record written on a device with no connection has nothing outside itself vouching for it. If it is lost, altered or simply never syncs, nothing anywhere contradicts the new version.

README · opening  ·  spec/04 §2.3

Weak time evidence

Offline systems record whatever the device clock said, and a device clock is a settable field. Vigilarch treats it as attacker-controlled input: no wall-clock read may enter the ledger at all, and hlc is a merge hint, never evidence.

invariant I4  ·  clippy.toml

Epistemic dishonesty

A gap in the record looks identical to a quiet shift. A system that interpolates across it, or reports a confident best estimate, has invented evidence. Vigilarch answers unwitnessed and shows the width of what it cannot account for.

invariant I5  ·  spec/02 §8.3

Cross-site isolation

Two sites that never meet each other cannot order their records against one another, and in wire version 1 a courier between them does not close that gap either. The honest output is incomparable — which is what this repository reports, and says so as an open item rather than a solved one.

spec/04 §5.2  ·  ADR-0003 Part 2

spec/04 §2.1 · the mechanism, in brief

Eight stages, and none of them is a clock.

Hover or focus a stage to light it and its neighbours. Where a stage has a live value under it, that value came back from the ledger running in this browser a moment ago — the same run the card at the top of the page printed.

  1. 01

    Observe

    A note is written on a device that may never have been online.

  2. 02

    Hash

    Deterministic CBOR, then BLAKE3. The identifier is the content.

  3. 03

    Sign

    Ed25519, domain-separated. A signature does not verify over the bare id.

    "vigilarch/1/sig" || 0x00 || idsrc/sign.rs — a constant, not a computed value
  4. 04

    Append

    seq and prev come from the store, not from the writer.

  5. 05

    Meet

    Two nodes come into contact. Nothing about wall-clock time is exchanged.

  6. 06

    Attest

    Each signs a statement about the other’s head: subject, seq, head.

  7. 07

    Entangle

    Observations and attestations become one DAG of causal-past edges.

  8. 08

    Verify

    bracket() returns sealed or unwitnessed — never a guess in between.

  1. 01

    Append

    Every node keeps a hash-linked chain of its own observations: entry n carries prev = the content address of entry n−1, and seq = n. A node cannot insert into its own past without either breaking a prev link or publishing two entries at one seq — both detectable.

    spec/01 §6.1, §6.6

  2. 02

    Meet

    When two nodes meet, each signs an attestation about the other’s current chain head: “I, the witness, saw the subject present head H at seq s.” The attestation says nothing in wall-clock terms. Each node embeds the attestation naming it as subject into its own next entry, via acks.

    spec/01 §6.5 · spec/02 §3.4

  3. 03

    Seal

    Attestations and observations form a DAG whose edges all mean “the tail is in the causal past of the head.” A record is sealed when an attestation by a witness other than its author provably follows it. Otherwise it is unwitnessed, and the window is left open rather than guessed at.

    spec/02 §4.2, §5.2

  4. 04

    Verify

    vigil-verify takes the evidence pack and the organisation’s public key, re-parses every object, rebuilds the chain and the DAG, and recomputes every bracket from scratch. It links only vigil-core — a second implementation written from the spec, not a call back into the code that built the pack. No database, no network, no node.

    spec/03 §5 · ADR-0005

The meeting

Contact between two devices. Each signs an attestation about the other’s current chain head — subject, seq, head — and nothing in wall-clock terms is exchanged. This is the only event in the system that creates time evidence.spec/01 §6.5 · spec/02 §3.4

Local ledger

Per-key hash chains behind a Store trait, with an in-memory backend always available so the ledger stays compilable to wasm32-unknown-unknown. Attestation DAG

Observations and attestations as vertices; every edge means “the tail is in the causal past of the head.” The graph is a pure function of the held set — the same objects in any ingest order give the identical DAG.spec/02 §4.2, §5.6

bracket()

Returns the upper bound U, the lower bound L, the witness depth and the unwitnessed window. Where the DAG does not establish an ordering the bound is None and the window is open on that side — never an interpolation. Adding a valid attestation may narrow a bracket; it may never widen one.spec/02 §5.5, §5.6

It links only vigil-core — not vigil-ledger, not even as a dev-dependency — so it is a second implementation written from the specification rather than a call back into the code that produced the pack. D

Entangle two independent histories

Observations and attestations form one directed graph in which every edge means “the tail is in the causal past of the head.” Once two chains share an attestation, facts about one constrain the other. The graph is a pure function of the held set: the same objects in any ingest order give the identical DAG.

The vertices are the entries and attestations state() returned. The chain edges are prev fields; the sealing edge is the upper_bound bracket() returned. No edge here is worked out in JavaScript — that would be a second implementation of the DAG, which invariant I2 forbids.

spec/02 §4.2, §5.6
live
E

Bound when a record was created — and show what it cannot bound

An attestation over a record proves it existed no later than that meeting. That is an upper bound, and it is the only kind an attestation gives. A lower bound, where one exists, is an ordering fact — “after that meeting” — and not a wall-clock time: turning it into one would need an external anchor, and v1 has none.

The axis below is causal order, not a clock, and carries no times for that reason. Bounded edges are solid; open edges are dashed and the fill fades out towards them, so an unbounded window cannot be misread as a tidy interval.

F

Catch one key signing two irreconcilable histories

Placing a record outside its bracket requires publishing a second entry at a seq the chain has already committed, or showing one witness a chain another witness’s attestation contradicts. Both are forks. A fork proof is self-verifying and attributes the collision to one key; quarantine then disputes that key’s unwitnessed records while leaving its pre-fork sealed ones valid.

The proof id, the collision label and the two colliding entry addresses below come from a real equivocate() call. The withheld sibling is the interesting one: no witness ever saw it, so it lands as disputed rather than being deleted — the dispute is itself evidence.

README · invariants

Six rules the code is not allowed to break.

I1

No node is the source of truth.

The hub is a convenience peer with no authority an edge node lacks. If the hub burns down, the organisation loses convenience and nothing else.

enforced by code and CI
I2

One implementation of the ledger.

Chain, attestation and verification logic exists once, in Rust, and stays compilable to wasm32-unknown-unknown. Storage lives behind a Store trait and an in-memory backend is always available.

enforced by code and CI
I3

Capture is immutable.

Observation and Attestation are never edited.

enforced by code and CI
I4

No wall-clock reads in the ledger.

Enforced by clippy.toml. All time flows through the HLC, which is a merge hint and never evidence. Device clocks are attacker-controlled input.

enforced by code and CI
I5

Never overstate what the system knows.

Any computed confidence is conservative one-sidedly, and every simulator run asserts it. Overstating knowledge is the cardinal defect of this system.

enforced by code and CI
I6

Conflicting values are never silently resolved.

If semantic interpretation is ever modelled, two supervisors disagreeing on a severity assessment is information, not a merge failure. v1 has no interpretation layer, so nothing today enforces this.

design commitment · nothing enforces it in v1

README · what this does not claim  ·  spec/04 §2.3

What it knows, and what it does not.

A guarantee that is not stated precisely is not a guarantee. The right-hand column is not fine print; it is half the claim, and it is given the same weight here as the left.

What Vigilarch knows

  • That a chain has not been rewritten. Entry n commits to entry n−1. A chain reports Verified, Incomplete or Violated, and an incomplete chain is never quietly upgraded to a verified one. spec/01 §6.6 · tests/chain.rs
  • That a record existed no later than a given meeting. A valid attestation by a witness other than the author, over a head at or past the record’s seq, places the record before everything that follows that attestation in the DAG. spec/02 §5.2
  • Which key equivocated. Two entries from one key at one seq yield a self-verifying fork proof naming the key. Quarantine then disputes that key’s unwitnessed records and leaves its pre-fork sealed ones valid. spec/02 §6.1, §6.4–6.5
  • How wide the span is that it cannot account for. The unwitnessed window is reported rather than hidden, and narrowing it by trusting a clock would be the cardinal defect. spec/02 §8.3 · invariant I5
  • That the answer does not depend on who is asking. The DAG is a pure function of the held set; the bracket is identical across ingest orders and byte-identical across machines, and adding a valid attestation can only narrow it. spec/02 §5.5, §5.6
  • That a third party can re-derive all of it. From the pack and a public key, with no database, no network and no node — and using code that does not link the code that built the pack. spec/03 §5 · ADR-0005

What Vigilarch does not know

  • Upper bound only. An attestation proves a record existed no later than the meeting. It cannot prove the record did not exist earlier. spec/02 §8.1
  • A node isolated since genesis is not detectable. It can fabricate a complete, signature-valid, internally consistent chain with any clock values. There is no contradiction to find. The correct output is a wide unwitnessed window — not an alarm, a detection, or a fork. spec/04 §2.3 · spec/02 §8.2
  • No interpolation, ever. Where the attestation graph does not prove an ordering, the answer is unwitnessed. Never a best estimate. invariant I5
  • No defence against a real-time lie. A person who reports something false as it happens produces a record that seals perfectly. Entanglement orders records; it does not make people honest. spec/02 §8.5
  • Witness depth counts keys, not people. One actor holding several device keys can attest their own records under “distinct” keys and inflate the depth. One key per org-issued device is an operational assumption, not a cryptographic guarantee, and is not implemented in v1. spec/04 §2.3 · spec/02 §8.9
  • Relative, not absolute. Every bound is relative to other chain events and, ultimately, to the moment vigil-verify runs. “Before Friday” is shorthand for a chain of relative orderings — there is no trusted clock anywhere in this system, and no external anchor in v1. spec/04 §2.3 · spec/03 §7.3, §7.4

And none of this is a new protocol. Hash-linked timestamping is Haber–Stornetta (1991); mutual attestation between peer histories is Maniatis & Baker’s timeline entanglement (2002). The contribution here is the application to disconnected incident reporting, the honest surfacing of what is not known, and the measurement — not the primitives. README · What this does not claim

spec/04-threat-model.md

Questions the threat model already answers.

Does Vigilarch detect backdating?

Not in general. An actor who sets a false hlc on an observation and appends it honestly to their own chain — correct prev, correct seq, no second entry at that position — produces a record that passes every check the system performs. The chain verifies. The bracket is computed correctly, and the false hlc sits inside that bracket where it changes no bound.

Backdating becomes catchable only when it additionally requires equivocation: inserting a record at a seq position the chain has already committed, or presenting one witness a chain that another witness’s attestation contradicts. Those are forks, and forks are detected and attributed.

spec/04 §4

What does a sealed verdict actually prove?

That the record provably existed no later than meeting U, and — where a lower bound exists — provably no earlier than meeting L in causal order. Backdating past U would require forging the witness’s key or having shown that witness a different chain, and the second is a fork.

It does not mean the record’s hlc is truthful.

spec/04 §2.2, §4

What happens to a node that has never met anyone?

It can fabricate a complete, signature-valid, internally consistent chain with any clock values, and there is no contradiction to find. The correct output is a wide unwitnessed window — open to genesis below, open to now above — labelled unwitnessed.

That is not an alarm, a detection, or a fork. The honest-partition scenario asserts exactly this: no alarm.

spec/04 §2.3  ·  spec/02 §8.2

Can a courier connect two sites that never meet each other?

Not in wire version 1. An Attestation carries the subject’s head but nothing about the witness’s own chain position, so the three edge rules produce no edge between two different authors’ chains. A mule that entangles Site A and then Site B issues two attestations, but nothing records the order it issued them in — the path Site A → mule → Site B does not exist in the graph.

Two sites connected only through a mule therefore produce incomparable records, which is the honest, conservative output rather than a correctness defect. The courier still tightens each site’s own brackets. Closing the gap needs a new witness_seq field and a fourth edge type, with its own ADR and a new golden vector.

spec/04 §5.2  ·  ADR-0003 Part 2

Does a witness depth of 3 mean three people vouched for this?

No — witness depth counts keys, not people. One actor holding several device keys can attest their own records under “distinct” keys and inflate the depth. The mitigation is one key per organisation-issued device, which is an operational assumption rather than a cryptographic guarantee, and it is not implemented in v1.

spec/04 §2.3  ·  spec/02 §8.9

So what does force a backdater into the open?

Within its bracket [L, U] a node may set a record’s hlc to any value; no bound depends on it. To place a record before its lower-bound meeting, the author must publish a second entry at an already-committed seq without that meeting in its acks. To place it after its upper-bound meeting, the author must have shown that witness a chain without the record.

Both are forks — detectable and attributable to a key the moment one verifier holds both branches. Escaping the bracket is not a subtle attack; it is a fork.

spec/04 §2.4  ·  spec/02 §5.4, §6.1

Is the protocol new?

No, and the repository is deliberate about saying so. Hash-linked timestamping is Haber–Stornetta (1991); mutual attestation between peer histories is Maniatis & Baker’s timeline entanglement (2002). The contribution is the application to disconnected incident reporting, the honest surfacing of what is not known, and the measurement.
